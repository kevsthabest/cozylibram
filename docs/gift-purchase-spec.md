# Gift a Book — Wishlist Purchasing & Gifting — Spec

**Status:** Draft for approval (2026-10-15, from the wishlist-purchasing exploration). Advisor design review returned AMEND (A1–A10); all amendments are folded in below, together with the User's rulings of 2026-10-15 (claim expiry confirmed, retailer set confirmed, A3 kept, A5 numbers accepted, A8 do it now, A9 as modified in §7), and the User's further ruling of 2026-10-15 that **gifting is opt-in per friend, recipient-controlled** (§6) — the grant that ruling adds is also what satisfies A9 for teen friends, replacing the separate teen exception (§7). One PR; the safety dependencies in §8 are ship-blockers, not follow-ups. Decisions in §1–§7 are settled from the design conversation and are specced as decided, not re-opened.
**Rubric snapshot:** Reader value — buying a friend the exact book they want, in the exact format they want it, stops being a research project: one tap stages everything, and nobody double-buys. Brief-fit — pass (circle-only, recipient controls their own data with an opt-in that defaults off, no counts or tallies shown to anyone, no productivity framing). Moat-fit — **high** (retailers can sell a book but cannot coordinate a circle around a private wishlist; the claims layer is the part only this app can own). Scope — **L**. Placement — **Free** (social core is free — standing constraint). Displaced surface — none; a Buy/Gift action on existing wishlist surfaces, one sheet, one settings panel section, one list in the buyer's own space.

## 1. What

A wishlist book becomes buyable — for a friend or for yourself — without the app ever becoming a store. The retailer is always the seller: no in-app checkout, no payments, no order data, no fulfillment, anywhere in v1. The app does the three things a retailer page can't:

1. **Knows what the recipient wants** — the exact book, in the exact format (the wishlist derivation: `status = 'tbr' && owned = 'tobuy'`, plus trophy wants via `wantFormat` on owned books).
2. **Coordinates the givers** — secret gift claims (§6) so two friends never buy the same gift and the surprise survives.
3. **Hands off with everything pre-staged** — one tap claims, reveals delivery details for copying, and opens the retailer's page for the exact edition.

**North star: minimum friction on the buyer's path.** The canonical flow (buyer gifting a friend — "the recipient"):

- The buyer taps **Buy/Gift** on a book on the recipient's Friend's Wishlist shelf (which already exists and already obeys the recipient's share/hide settings). The action exists at all only because the recipient has granted this friend permission to gift them (§6) — gifting is opt-in per friend, and an ungranted friend sees the plain wishlist exactly as it works today.
- That **one tap**: (a) silently creates the buyer's gift claim — no separate "claim" step, no dialog; (b) opens the **"Send this gift" sheet** showing the recipient's delivery details with **one-tap copy buttons** — per field, plus one button copying a full formatted address block ready to paste into a checkout form; (c) presents **one prominent button** that opens the retailer page for the exact edition (deep link built from ISBN-13).
- **Retailer choice is pre-decided, not asked every time.** The link is steered by the wanted format: physical → the buyer's remembered preferred print retailer; ebook → the Kobo or Kindle gift page; audio → Libro.fm. Region-aware, Canada defaults in v1 (Indigo / Amazon.ca / Kobo). The buyer can switch retailer inside the sheet; the choice is remembered per format as a roamed preference.
- Back in the app, the claim waits in the buyer's **"Gifts you're giving"** list with a one-tap **Mark purchased**. Claims auto-release when the recipient acquires the book themselves (the app never tells the buyer why a claim released — it just frees the book), and expire after 45 days if never purchased (see §6).

**Honest bound, stated plainly:** the retailer's own checkout — their sign-in, their payment — is the irreducible remainder. The app removes everything on its side of the handoff; it cannot and does not try to remove theirs.

**Own-list buying** uses the same link machinery minus claims and address: Buy on your own wishlist (or on a trophy want in your own library) opens a minimal sheet — steered retailer, one button. No claim exists because there is nobody to coordinate with and nothing to hide from yourself.

## 2. Who

Circle friends and family buying for each other — birthdays, holidays, "saw this and thought of you" — plus the reader buying their own wishlist efficiently. The recipient pool is exactly the people whose wishlists are already visible in the Circle today; the gifting pool for any given recipient is the subset of those friends the recipient has opted in, one by one (§6).

## 3. Why now

The foundations landed in the last week: the wishlist unification made "wishlist book" a single derivation every surface agrees on (v559), format + trophy wants made the *wanted edition* explicit data (v563), handles made circle members addressable (v550), and Marginalia proved the zero-policy-table + definer-RPC pattern for sensitive circle data (v574). Gifting is the first feature that composes all four. It also closes the loop the Friend's Wishlist shelf opened when it shipped as a viewing surface: viewing was the promise, buying is the point.

## 4. Surfaces

- **Buy/Gift action** on the Friend's Wishlist shelf (per book), and on trophy wants shown there — **rendered only for friends the recipient has granted gifting permission** (§6); everyone else sees the shelf exactly as it renders today, with no action and no claim states. If a book is already claimed, the action reads **"Claimed"** and is inert (§6).
- **Buy action** on the user's own Wishlist shelf and on trophy wants in their own library — same machinery, minimal sheet.
- **"Send this gift" sheet** (buyer, after the tap): recipient delivery details (§7) with copy buttons, the steered retailer button, a retailer switcher, and the claim's state (active since, expires). Swipe-down-to-dismiss per the app rule, like every sheet.
- **"Gifts you're giving" list**: its own small view off the Circle (ruled for v1 — not a section on the buyer's Wishlist shelf; the shelf is about wanting, the list is about giving): active claims with book, recipient, expiry, **Mark purchased**, and **Release claim**. A 3D-shelf presentation of this list is a possible future, explicitly out of v1 scope. For the recipient, receiving is not a surface — the only recipient-side trace of claims anywhere is the opt-in setting below.
- **Settings → Circle & privacy**: the recipient-side controls — the per-friend gifting grants (**"Allow <friend> to gift me"**, default OFF for every friendship, §6) managed alongside the other per-friend circle privacy controls, the sharing toggle (default OFF) and address edit/delete — beside the existing Marginalia toggle, following its pattern. The same panel carries the reveal **access log** (§7, two-tier visibility, pull-only — no push, no badge) and the recipient claim-visibility setting (§6, default OFF).

## 5. Retailer links

- Deep links are **plain URLs built from ISBN-13** (product page where the retailer exposes a stable ISBN URL, search-by-ISBN otherwise). Fallback when a record has no ISBN: title + author search URL. No prices are displayed in-app, ever — the app has no price source and must not imply one.
- **No affiliates in v1.** Links carry no tags, no tracking parameters, no referral codes. The link builder is the single place retailer URLs are composed, so a future tag seam is a one-spot change — noted here as a future possibility only, not a v1 design element.
- Retailer set (v1, Canada, **confirmed**): **Indigo** and **Amazon.ca** (print), **Kobo** and **Kindle/Amazon.ca** (ebook gift pages), **Libro.fm** (audio). The per-format default is remembered per buyer (roamed preference), switchable in the sheet.

## 6. Secret gift claims

- **Gifting is opt-in per friend (User's ruling, 2026-10-15).** A per-(recipient, friend) grant — **"Allow <friend> to gift me"** — is controlled by the recipient and defaults to **OFF for every friendship**: nobody can gift a recipient until that recipient enables them. The grant is the base permission underneath the entire gifting surface for that pair, and underneath every other predicate in §6–§7. Without it: no Buy/Gift action renders for that friend on that recipient's wishlist, the claim RPC refuses creation with the identical shape as every other claim refusal, and no claim states are served to that friend — they see the plain wishlist exactly as it works today. The grant is evaluated server-side inside the claim, state, and reveal RPCs, like every other predicate; a client that renders the action anyway gains nothing.
- **One active claim per (recipient, book).** The first Buy/Gift tap locks it; every other friend sees the book as "Claimed" with the action inert. The lock is what makes double-buying structurally impossible rather than socially discouraged.
- **Claimer caps (Advisor A2):** at most **2 active claims per (claimer, recipient)** and at most **10 active claims per claimer globally**. Without caps, one friend could claim every book on a wishlist in a minute and freeze that person's entire gifting surface for the claim lifetime. Caps are enforced inside the claim RPC; a cap refusal returns the same identical result as every other claim refusal.
- **Claim-state visibility composes at serve time (Advisor A7):** claim states are computed against the world as it is at the moment of the read — accepted link, no block in either direction, wishlist visible, **gifting grant in force** — mirroring the reveal RPC's per-call composition (§7). Losing the link, being blocked, or **having the grant revoked** instantly stops a friend's locks from showing on any surface and from grounding reveals, everywhere, with no cleanup pass. **Grant revocation mid-claim** composes the same way: the claim stops showing (including in the claimer's own list, where it reads as released at serve time, freeing the book for other granted friends) and grounds no reveal from the moment of revocation; re-granting does not resurrect it — the friend claims again.
- **Visibility is exactly three-way asymmetric:**
  - The **claimer** sees their claims in "Gifts you're giving."
  - The recipient's **other granted friends** (friends holding the grant above) see only an anonymous **"Claimed"** state on that book. They do **not** see who claimed, when, or whether it was later purchased. The friends' state RPC payload is **pinned to `book_key` + `state` only** (Advisor A6) — no timestamps, no claimer, no counts — so the anonymity promise is enforced by the payload shape, not by client politeness. Friends **without** the grant are served no claim states at all: for them the shelf is the plain wishlist, indistinguishable from the feature not existing.
  - The **recipient has no read path at all by default** — not counts, not states, not an empty-state that differs from "feature off." The friend-facing state RPC refuses the owner by construction (caller ≠ owner, accepted link, wishlist visible), so the recipient cannot enumerate claim state through any door — unless they turn on the setting below.
- **Recipient claim-visibility setting (User's addition, post-Advisor-review; gets Advisor eyes again at build review):** a recipient may opt in via **"See claims on my wishlist"** (Settings → Circle & privacy, **default OFF**, surprise preserved) to see claims on **their own** wishlist books, including who claimed. The read path is a **dedicated recipient RPC that exists only when the setting is on** — server-enforced, not a client flag: with the setting off, the recipient's zero-read-path posture above is unchanged and total. The opt-in view's payload is fuller than the friends' view (book + claimer + state) but still carries **no address data** — claims never carry any. Interplay: A6's minimal payload governs friends' views only; this setting does not loosen what any friend can see.
- **Lifecycle:** `active → purchased` (buyer marks it) `| released` (buyer lets go, the recipient acquires the book — evaluated against the recipient's *current* wishlist derivation at serve time, never by a stored snapshot — or the recipient revokes the claimer's gifting grant, composed the same way at serve time) `| expired` (45 days unpurchased). Released/expired claims free the book immediately and silently.
- Claims work identically on trophy wants: wanting the hardcover of a book you own as an ebook is a giftable state, and claiming it follows the same rules.
- Claims carry **no text, no message, no note field** — there is nothing user-authored to moderate (see §8 on the shield).

## 7. The gift address vault

The security core of the feature. A postal address is the most sensitive data the app has ever held; it is treated accordingly.

- **Storage:** a dedicated table, **off `profiles`** — parcel name, street, city, province/state, postal code, country, optional phone (couriers sometimes require one), plus an optional **gift email** for digital gifts (steered ebook/audio gifts reveal the email under the same gate, for pasting into the retailer's gift flow). Owner-only RLS on the table itself; **no friend read policy exists.**
- **Encryption at build (Advisor A8 — Decision: do it now):** the vault's address, email, and phone columns are encrypted at the column level with **pgsodium**, from the moment the table is created. Rationale: the table is empty at creation, so encrypting now is the cheapest it will ever be — retrofitting means a migration over the most sensitive live data the app owns; and this team's workflow runs production SQL routinely, so column encryption is what makes an accidental `SELECT` of this table, a dumped backup, or a leaked credential a non-event for addresses specifically. Keys live in **Supabase Vault**, never in the repo and never in the client; decryption happens only inside the reveal RPC.
- **Sharing toggle:** a per-owner switch, **default OFF**. Off means: no reveals, to anyone, for any reason — the feature behaves for that recipient as if no address exists.
- **The reveal:** exactly one SECURITY DEFINER RPC, and it is **claim-gated**. It returns delivery details only when **all** hold at call time, in this order: **(1) the owner has granted this caller gifting (§6) — the base permission underneath every other predicate in this section**; caller ↔ owner have an accepted circle link; no block in either direction; the owner's sharing toggle is on; the caller holds an **active claim** on a wishlist book of the owner; the first-pair claim-age rule below is satisfied; and the pair budgets below are not exhausted. (There is no separate minors predicate left in this list: for a teen caller, the grant check *is* the minors check — see the minors bullet below.) Every refusal is the **identical empty result** — no error shape distinguishes "no grant," "no address," "sharing off," "no claim," "blocked," "too young a claim," or "budget spent" (the anti-oracle discipline of the handle RPCs).
- **Field-scoped by gift type (Advisor A4):** the reveal returns only what the claimed gift needs. A **print** claim reveals the postal fields + phone. An **ebook/audio** claim reveals **only the gift email** — never the postal address. Least disclosure per gift, enforced inside the RPC by the claim's wanted format.
- **First-pair claim age (Advisor A3 — kept):** the **first** reveal between a (viewer, owner) pair requires the grounding claim to be **≥ 24 hours old**. A pair with any prior *resolved* claim reveals immediately. This keeps the canonical one-tap flow frictionless for established gifters and puts the only wait exactly where harvesting lives: a novel pair interaction, where a would-be harvester must hold a socially visible "Claimed" lock for a full day before extracting anything.
- **Reveal budgets (Advisor A1):** pair-scoped and enforced inside the reveal RPC — at most **3 reveals per claim** (re-copying after a typo or a lost checkout tab is the legitimate repeat) and at most **10 reveals per (viewer, owner) pair per rolling 30 days**. The pair cap deliberately does **not** reset when claims are released and re-created — claim-cycling cannot mint a fresh budget, because the address is the same no matter which book grounded the claim. (This supersedes the draft's borrowed handle-lookups rate; a home address is not an existence probe and is not budgeted like one.)
- **One generic line for every refusal (Advisor A10):** whatever the cause — toggle off, no address stored, claim too young, budget spent, blocked, anything — the "Send this gift" sheet shows a single line: **"Delivery details aren't shared for this gift."** No client-side distinguishing of causes, ever.
- **Access log, two-tier visibility (Advisor A5):** every successful reveal is logged (viewer, owner, claim, timestamp) at the moment it happens — recording is immediate; only *visibility* is tiered. **Tier 1 — the fact:** after **72 hours**, the owner can see that a reveal happened (count + timestamp only — no claimer, no book), pull-only in Settings → Circle & privacy, no push and no badge: an owner who looks can detect; an owner who doesn't keeps the surprise. **Tier 2 — the detail:** claimer identity and book become owner-visible at **claim resolution or 14 days after the reveal, whichever comes first**. Both tiers are enforced server-side inside the owner-log RPC, so a modified client cannot pull detail early. **Admins read the log immediately** — abuse response never waits on owner reports or claim resolution. Rationale: the draft's resolve-only visibility let the claimer control the blind window simply by holding the claim (up to the full 45-day claim life); an address is used physically within days, so the blind window is now short and bounded, and only the *detail* waits.
- **Owner control:** edit or delete anytime; delete is a **hard delete**; no address history is kept anywhere; the row cascades away on account deletion.
- **Minors (Advisor A9, as resolved by the per-friend grant):** address **storage** is disabled for teen accounts (age band), no exceptions in v1 — the settings panel does not offer the field. Beyond that, there is **no separate teen mechanism**: the earlier draft's per-pair teen exception is **subsumed by the §6 gifting grant** and removed. For a teen friend, the recipient granting "Allow <friend> to gift me" *is* the owner-consent act A9 required — the consent belongs to the address owner (a parent enables their own child to gift them) rather than the platform deciding which families may ship each other parcels. Teen default-deny holds structurally, because the grant defaults OFF for every friendship: a teen without the grant cannot claim or reveal at all; a teen **with** the grant claims and reveals like anyone, subject to every budget and gate in this section. Claiming **for** a teen recipient follows the same uniform rule as claiming for anyone — it needs the teen recipient's own grant (which the teen controls, e.g. granting a parent) — and the recipient side still needs no address: the storage rule means the reveal is empty, and the generic A10 line covers it.
- **Honest bound, stated plainly:** access can be governed; a legitimate claimer's memory cannot. Once a genuine claimer has seen an address to ship a parcel, no software can unshow it. The design guarantees nobody else sees it, nobody sees it without an active reason, every view is recorded, and access dies with the friendship, the block, the toggle, or the claim.

## 8. Dependencies — ship-blockers

The feature is **not visible to anyone** unless all of these hold:

- **Block severs everything, immediately:** blocking (either direction) kills claim visibility and address access; the reveal RPC re-checks blocks on every call — nothing is cached open.
- **Recipient wishlist-hide governs the whole surface**, exactly as it governs the Friend's Wishlist shelf today: hidden wishlist ⇒ no Buy/Gift actions, no claim states, no reveals.
- **No grant, no surface (per pair):** the gifting surface exists for a (recipient, friend) pair only while the recipient's §6 grant stands. An ungranted friend gets no action, no claim, and no claim states — for that pair the feature does not exist, and the wishlist renders exactly as it does today.
- **Fail closed until the migration is applied:** the client ships dark; absent the tables/RPCs, no surface renders (the Marginalia pattern). The migration ships in the PR and is applied post-merge.
- **Server-side enforcement is the ship shape:** every gate in §6–§7 — the per-friend gifting grant (which also carries the minors rule, §7) and the teen storage ban, claimer caps, pair budgets, first-pair claim age, field-scoping, log tiers, the recipient visibility setting — is evaluated inside the definer RPCs. A client that skips or fakes a check gains nothing; the build is not done if any predicate lives only in the UI.
- **The content shield is not applicable here, by construction:** claims carry no user-authored content, addresses never cross to any surface but the claim-gated sheet, and the only pre-existing data involved (the wishlist itself) already passes through the established shelf-visibility path. There is nothing for the shield to hold. Stated explicitly so the absence is a decision, not an omission.

## 9. Data model (sketch)

- `gift_claims` — id, recipient_id, claimer_id, book_key (the canonical key other circle surfaces use), state, created_at, resolved_at. **Zero-policy table** (admin SELECT only, the Marginalia shape); all access via definer RPCs: `gift_claim` (create/lock — enforces the recipient's gifting grant to the caller (§6), the one-active-claim lock, and the A2 claimer caps; identical result whether the refusal is a missing grant, a lock, a cap, a block, or a hidden wishlist), `gift_claim_states_for` (granted friends' anonymous view; serves nothing at all to ungranted friends; payload pinned to `book_key` + `state` only per A6; refuses the owner; composes link/block/wishlist/grant state at serve time per A7), `gift_claims_for_recipient` (the §6 opt-in recipient view — refuses unless the caller's own visibility setting is on; payload book + claimer + state, never any address data), `gift_claims_mine`, `gift_mark_purchased`, `gift_release`. Uniqueness of one active claim per (recipient, book) enforced in the claim RPC under a per-recipient advisory lock.
- `gift_addresses` — owner_id (PK), the address fields, gift_email, sharing_enabled (default false), updated_at. Address/email/phone columns are **pgsodium-encrypted** (A8; keys in Supabase Vault). Owner-only RLS; friend access solely via `gift_address_reveal` (§7), which is also where the §6 gifting grant (predicate #1), the A1 pair budgets, the A3 first-pair claim-age check, and the A4 field-scoping are evaluated. There is no A9-specific predicate left to evaluate: for a teen caller, the grant check *is* the minors check. Reveal counting for the pair budgets reuses the send-log machinery pattern, keyed on the (viewer, owner) pair so claim-cycling cannot reset it.
- `gift_grants` — recipient_id, friend_id, created_at (the §6 per-friend gifting grants; recipient-controlled, default absent = OFF, revocable by row delete; consulted inside `gift_claim`, `gift_claim_states_for`, and `gift_address_reveal`). This table **replaces** the earlier draft's `gift_address_grants`: the A9 teen exception no longer exists as a separate mechanism, because this grant subsumes it (§7).
- `gift_address_access_log` — viewer_id, owner_id, claim_id, revealed_at. Owner reads their own log via a definer RPC enforcing §7's two tiers server-side: fact (count + timestamp) only once a reveal is 72h old; claimer + book only once the claim has resolved or the reveal is 14 days old, whichever first. Admins read the full log immediately via the admin policy path.
- **Server-held settings:** the recipient claim-visibility setting (§6) lives server-side (a `profiles` column in the `marginalia_enabled` pattern — authoritative for serving, not projected into the circle profile view), because the recipient RPC must enforce it without trusting the client; the gifting grants likewise live server-side (`gift_grants` above), because three RPCs must enforce them without trusting the client. **Roamed preference:** the buyer's per-format retailer choice joins the prefs section (js/229 pattern — never the frozen settings list). Claims and addresses themselves are cloud-only state, never local.

## 10. Testing & verification

- Suites pin: the **grant matrix** (no grant ⇒ the Buy/Gift action is absent for that friend, `gift_claim` refuses with the identical shape as every other refusal, and `gift_claim_states_for` serves that friend nothing — the plain wishlist; grant in force ⇒ the full surface works for that pair only; **grant revoked mid-claim** ⇒ at serve time the claim vanishes from friends' states and from the claimer's own list (reading as released), the book frees for other granted friends, and the claim no longer grounds a reveal; re-granting does not resurrect the claim); the claim lock (second claim refused, identically shaped); the **claimer caps** (third active claim against one recipient refused; eleventh global active claim refused — both identically shaped); the friends' state RPC refusing the owner, its payload carrying `book_key` + `state` and nothing else, and a claimer's locks vanishing from friends' views the moment the link drops or a block lands (serve-time composition); the full reveal matrix (no link / blocked / toggle off / no claim / claim resolved ⇒ identical empty; all conditions met ⇒ details); the **pair budgets** (fourth reveal on one claim refused; eleventh reveal within a pair's rolling 30 days refused; release-and-reclaim does not restore budget); the **first-pair claim-age rule** (a novel pair's reveal at 23h59m refused identically, at 24h allowed; a pair with a prior resolved claim reveals immediately on a fresh claim); the **field-scoping matrix** (print claim ⇒ postal + phone, no email; ebook/audio claim ⇒ gift email only, no postal field present in the payload); the **two-tier log** (owner sees nothing of a reveal before 72h; fact-only — count + timestamp, no claimer/book — from 72h until resolution-or-14-days; full detail at resolution and at 14 days, whichever first; admin reads immediately); the **generic sheet line** (A10) rendered for every refusal cause in the matrix with no distinguishing text, pinned beside the RPC refusal matrix; auto-release against a changed wishlist derivation; the **minors matrix** (teen cannot store an address; teen without the recipient's grant gets no claim and no reveal — the refusal identical in shape to a missing grant for anyone; teen with the grant claims and reveals under the standard predicates, budgets included; revoking the grant mid-claim kills the claim's visibility and reveal grounding at serve time; claiming *for* a teen recipient requires the teen's own grant like anyone's, with the reveal empty by the storage rule); the **recipient visibility setting** (OFF ⇒ the recipient RPC refuses/the recipient reads zero claim data through every door; ON ⇒ the recipient reads claims on their own books only — book + claimer + state, no address data, and nothing about books they don't own); the link builder's format steering, per-format remembered retailer, and ISBN-missing fallback; fail-closed rendering with the migration absent.
- **Acceptance is pair verification** with two linked circle test accounts (the established pair-verification discipline): B grants A (and a third friend) gifting permission — an ungranted fourth friend sees no action and no claim states at any point; A claims B's book; B's other granted friend sees "Claimed" and not who; B sees nothing anywhere (visibility setting off); A's reveal returns the address while the claim is active and stops the moment A releases it, B blocks A, or B revokes A's grant; B's access log shows the reveal's fact after 72h and its detail only after the claim resolves. Coordinator spot-checks the RPC matrix RLS-faithfully against the real circle pair before the feature is called live.
- Standard gates otherwise: full suite at the CONSTRAINTS baseline, Advisor review as the merge gate. Sound-free feature — the only device-owed check is sheet feel on the Pixel.

## 11. Legal notes (brief; launch-era, not alpha blockers; not legal advice)

- The design keeps the app **out of seller-of-record territory**: no payment is taken, no order is placed, no goods are promised by the app — the retailer's contract is with the buyer. That single fact avoids sales-tax collection, distance-selling contract rules, and payment-card compliance scope.
- Address storage is a **launch-checklist item**: privacy policy must name the purpose, retention, and processors (PIPEDA; Quebec's Law 25 for Quebec users); a breach-response plan must exist before public launch; the minors carve-out and column-level encryption (A8) are already in the design (§7) rather than deferred to policy or a launch retrofit.
- Affiliate disclosure duties (Competition Act / FTC) attach **only if affiliates ever arrive** — v1's tagless links carry none. If the future tag seam is ever used, disclosure copy ships in the same change, not after.

## 12. Explicitly out of scope

- Affiliates, tags, referral codes, price display (see §5, §11).
- In-app checkout, payments, order tracking, delivery status — permanently the retailer's, not deferred features.
- Group gifts and cost-splitting (a plausible v2; it reopens the who-claimed question in §13 rather than being smuggled into v1).
- Retailer wishlist sync or mirroring — **rejected as load-bearing**: no add-to-wishlist API exists at any major retailer, and two lists diverge. The in-app wishlist is the only list.
- Followers, public profiles, anyone outside the accepted circle — **ever**. This feature is circle-only permanently, not circle-only-for-now.
- An address book for non-gifting purposes; addresses for anyone but the owner themselves; more than one stored address per owner in v1.
- Gift messages, cards, wrap options, or any free-text field on a claim.

## 13. Open questions

Ruled and closed on 2026-10-15 (recorded here so the closures aren't lost): claim expiry **45 days** (§6); retailer starter set **confirmed** (§5); gift-email surfacing in the sheet **ships in v1** (§7's field-scoping presupposes it); "Gifts you're giving" is **its own small view off the Circle** (§4); **gifting is opt-in per friend, recipient-controlled** (§6) — and with it the A9 minors asymmetry is closed by subsumption: the grant is the owner-consent act for a teen friend, and the earlier separate teen-exception proposal is retired (§7). What remains genuinely open:

- **Should other friends eventually see *who* claimed?** V1 says anonymous for friends (the recipient's own opt-in view, §6, is a separate matter). Naming the claimer to friends is what a group-gift feature would need — deferred until that feature is a real proposal, not assumed here.

## 14. Veto points

- The **per-friend opt-in grant** (§6) — the User's ruling; default OFF for every friendship is the standing decision, and any future move toward a circle-wide default would be a fresh ruling, not a tweak.
- The **per-format retailer defaults** (§5) — the starter set is confirmed, but which retailer each format steers to first remains the User's pick.
- The **claim expiry length** (45 days, §6) and the **friends-anonymous claim rule** (§6) — both reversible in one line each if lived experience disagrees. The recipient claim-visibility setting (§6) is the User's own addition and ships default OFF regardless.
- The **two-tier access-log visibility** (§7) — the 72h fact tier and the resolution-or-14-days detail tier are accepted numbers; the *principle* (a short, bounded blind window; only detail waits) is the standing decision if the numbers are ever revisited.
- The **first-pair 24h claim age** (§7, A3) — kept at review; reversible if it proves to bite real first-time gifters more than it deters harvesting.
- The **sheet's copy** — "Send this gift," the claimed state wording, the generic refusal line (§7, A10), and the sharing toggle's plain-language explanation ("friends who claim one of your wishlist books can see this address to ship it to you") are all the User's to veto at the build's review pass.
